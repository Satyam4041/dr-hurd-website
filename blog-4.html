How Can I Tell If Neurofeedback Is Right for Me?

Sep 28, 2026·8 min read

By Dr. Merlyn Hurd, PhD

If you have been looking into neurofeedback, you may be asking, Is neurofeedback right for me? The answer is not a simple yes or no. Not everyone needs neurofeedback, and it is not a quick fix or a replacement for other forms of treatment. Whether it may be suitable depends on your needs, history, and goals.

Neurofeedback is a non-invasive training method that uses information about brain activity to provide feedback. EEG sensors pick up electrical activity, and a person receives feedback during a specially designed activity. With practice, they can learn to notice and regulate certain patterns.

Start With the Problem, Not the Solution

Difficulty concentrating can have many possible causes. Someone may not be getting enough sleep, may be experiencing stress or anxiety, or may have had concentration difficulties for a long time. The same symptom can come from very different situations. Understanding what is behind it can help a person consider the next step.

Neurofeedback is one component of a more comprehensive, individualized plan that starts with assessment and treatment planning. A conversation with a doctor or mental health professional can help you understand the options available to you.

Who Might Explore Neurofeedback?

People may look into neurofeedback when they are interested in support with attention and concentration, sleep, stress and relaxation, anxiety, emotional regulation, mental fatigue, or other cognitive and behavioral concerns. Having one of these concerns does not necessarily mean neurofeedback should be used. A professional can help assess whether it is appropriate for your situation.

Why Personalization Matters

There are no two brains that are exactly alike. Even if two people describe the same symptom, their circumstances may be very different. One person who is unable to sleep may be working an irregular schedule; another may be suffering from constant anxiety. Applying the same treatment to everyone may not make sense.

A personalized approach begins with questions about the individual: what is happening, what has already been tried, and what they would like to change. Symptoms, background, routines, and clinical assessment can all inform a care plan.

What Does QEEG Have to Do With Neurofeedback?

QEEG stands for quantitative electroencephalography. It records and analyzes brainwave activity using EEG technology. QEEG brain mapping can provide additional information about activity patterns that may be considered along with a person’s background, symptoms, and clinical evaluation.

A QEEG is not a crystal ball and cannot be used by itself to diagnose mental health disorders. It is one piece of information that may help professionals understand the bigger picture.

What Research Can Tell Us

Neurofeedback has been studied for attention, anxiety, sleep, and other mental and cognitive concerns. The quality of research varies by topic. Some findings are encouraging, while systematic reviews also describe limitations in study quality and differences between protocols. It is better to avoid promises that neurofeedback helps everybody. It may be an option for some people, and expectations should be discussed with a qualified provider.

For further reading, see the systematic review of biofeedback and neurofeedback for anxiety disorders, which discusses both findings and limitations in the available studies.

Questions to Ask Before Starting

Before choosing a provider, consider asking:

  • What can I expect during the initial assessment?
  • Will QEEG be included, and what will it measure?
  • How will my plan be personalized?
  • How will progress be assessed, and what happens if there is no progress?
  • How many sessions might be involved?
  • What other treatment options may fit my goals?

Ask about the therapist’s background, assessment methods, services, and how progress is tracked. A consultation lets you share your experience, ask questions, and decide whether to proceed.

Neurofeedback and Other Care

Choosing neurofeedback does not automatically mean setting aside another kind of treatment. A person may have therapy at the same time or may continue prescribed medication while undergoing training. Treatment does not have to be either-or; the plan depends on what suits the person best.

If you take prescribed medication, do not abruptly discontinue or change it in favor of neurofeedback. Speak with the healthcare professional who prescribed it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is neurofeedback for everyone?

No. Its relevance depends on the needs of each particular case.

Can neurofeedback treat anxiety or ADD?

Studies have been carried out, but findings vary and more research may be needed. A provider can discuss the evidence and its limits.

Do I need QEEG before neurofeedback?

It depends on the clinic. Ask how the assessment information will be used in planning your care.

Does QEEG diagnose mental health disorders?

No. QEEG provides information about brainwave activity; it is not a separate diagnostic tool.

Can I have neurofeedback if I take medication?

Talk with your healthcare professionals. Do not change medication without consulting the prescriber.

The First Step Is Understanding Your Options

When you are facing concentration problems, insomnia, stress, anxiety, or another concern, it is natural to hope for a quick solution. A useful starting point is to understand your symptoms, past experiences, and goals. You do not have to figure it out alone; a proper consultation can help you consider the next step.
