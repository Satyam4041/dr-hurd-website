Neurofeedback vs. Medication for Anxiety: Easy to Understand

Sep 28, 2026·7 min read

By Dr. Merlyn Hurd, PhD

If anxiety is interfering with sleep, focus, relationships, work, or daily functioning, you may be considering possible treatment options. A common question is whether neurofeedback is better than medication for anxiety. There is no easy answer. Medication and neurofeedback are different approaches, and the suitable choice depends on a person’s symptoms, medical history, and goals.

For some people, medication can be part of treatment. For others, therapy, neurofeedback, lifestyle changes, or another approach may be considered. A healthcare professional can help you understand the options.

What Is Medication for Anxiety?

Medication may help decrease anxiety symptoms by affecting chemicals in the brain. Depending on individual circumstances, healthcare professionals may prescribe SSRIs, SNRIs, buspirone, anti-anxiety medication, or other medicines. Some medications may take several weeks to show their maximum effects, and some can have side effects. A medical professional should decide whether medication is right for you and monitor its use.

Medication can be useful for many patients, but it may not be the only method. For current information about medication classes used for mental health conditions, see the National Institute of Mental Health guide to mental health medications.

What Is Neurofeedback?

Neurofeedback is a type of brain-based training using EEG data for real-time feedback. Sensors record brain activity, and a computer gives feedback based on that activity. With repeated sessions, the feedback is intended to support learning and regulation of certain patterns of brain activity.

At NY Neurofeedback, the process starts with assessment and QEEG brain mapping. The clinic describes its approach as Assess, Plan, Treat. QEEG information may help develop an individualized neurofeedback protocol. QEEG should not be used as a stand-alone diagnostic tool for anxiety; clinical history and assessment are still important.

Neurofeedback vs. Medication: The Basic Difference

A general comparison of medication and neurofeedback for anxiety
MedicationNeurofeedback
Uses medication to influence biological processesUses real-time EEG feedback as part of brain training
Prescribed and monitored by a medical professionalConducted through structured neurofeedback sessions
May help reduce anxiety symptomsAims to train regulation of brain activity
Effects and side effects can vary between peopleProtocols can be individualized based on assessment

This does not mean one approach is automatically better. They are simply different methods, and sometimes both are used.

Where Does QEEG Come Into Play?

QEEG, or quantitative electroencephalography, measures and analyzes brainwave activity using reference data. A care plan may consider not only symptoms, but also information about brain activity.

QEEG can offer additional information that may help develop a personalized treatment strategy. It must not be treated as an independent diagnostic tool for anxiety. Clinical history and assessment remain vital.

What Has Research Found?

Research into neurofeedback treatment for anxiety is promising in some areas and still emerging. Reviews have suggested that biofeedback techniques may benefit anxiety, while also pointing to gaps in the literature and the need for more controlled research. Some EEG neurofeedback reviews report positive results, but methodological limitations make it difficult to draw strong conclusions.

Neurofeedback may be a treatment choice to discuss, but it should not be considered a guaranteed solution or a substitute for established care.

For context on the limits of the evidence, see this systematic review of biofeedback and neurofeedback for anxiety disorders.

Can Neurofeedback and Medication Be Used Together?

Some people receive medication while also participating in psychotherapy or neurofeedback. Not all treatments need to be either-or choices. Coordination among the professionals involved can help keep care aligned with a person’s needs.

Never stop, lower, or change anxiety medication without speaking with the healthcare professional who prescribed it.

Questions to Discuss With a Professional

  • How are my symptoms affecting everyday life?
  • How long have I had these concerns?
  • Are sleep and concentration issues part of the picture?
  • What are my treatment objectives?
  • Could I benefit from a clinical or neurological assessment?
  • How would we measure progress?

Frequently Asked Questions

Which is more effective for anxiety: neurofeedback or medication?

There is no definite answer for everyone. Each treatment approach works differently, and there is no universal treatment that works equally well for all people.

Can neurofeedback replace anxiety medication?

Not necessarily. If you are currently taking a prescription, discuss this with your healthcare provider before making any changes.

Is neurofeedback associated with medication?

No. Neurofeedback records brain activity with sensors and provides feedback during training.

Does QEEG diagnose anxiety?

No. QEEG provides information about brain activity that may be considered alongside clinical history and evaluation.

Where can I find neurofeedback in NYC?

NY Neurofeedback offers individualized neurofeedback sessions using QEEG as part of its process. Visit the consultation page to ask about the approach.

Consider the Person, Not Just the Symptom

Every person experiences anxiety differently. Medication may be useful for some; psychotherapy may help people understand and change patterns of thought and behavior; neurofeedback offers another approach using real-time EEG feedback. Learning about your symptoms and goals is part of considering what may be appropriate for you.
