Biofeedback and Neurofeedback

Difference Between Biofeedback and Neurofeedback

Jul 17 · 7 min read

Biofeedback vs Neurofeedback: Quick Answer

Biofeedback and neurofeedback are two non-invasive therapies that can help people learn more about physiological functions and practice regulating some of them. The basic difference lies in the part of the body they focus on. Biofeedback pays attention to heart rate, muscle tension, breathing, and skin temperature, whereas neurofeedback targets brain activity measured via electroencephalography (EEG).

Even though the techniques are similar, they can be applied for different purposes. Biofeedback is used to teach people to better recognize and manage physiological stress responses, while neurofeedback aims to support healthy brain activity related to concentration, emotional regulation, sleep, and mental performance.

What Is Biofeedback?

Biofeedback is a therapeutic process in which a person gains a better understanding of the functions of their body. Physiological measurements such as heart rate, muscle tension, breathing, skin temperature, and sweat-gland activity are made using special sensors and displayed on a monitor. This helps show how the body reacts to factors such as stress and relaxation.

Through practice and awareness, individuals can improve body self-regulation with the help of these techniques.

Biofeedback has been applied in wellness and healthcare facilities for many years. It does not require medication and can be personalized to an individual’s goals.

What Is Neurofeedback?

Neurofeedback, also known as EEG biofeedback, is a specific type of biofeedback technique that works with brain activity. In contrast with other types of biofeedback that monitor muscle activity or heart rate, neurofeedback uses EEG devices to monitor brainwave activity through electrodes attached to the scalp.

During neurofeedback therapy, an individual may watch a movie, animation, or visual display while brainwave activity is monitored. The software detects desired patterns of brain activity and provides an immediate signal when those patterns occur, reinforcing the training process.

Neurofeedback therapy does not address symptoms directly. It is intended to support the brain’s development of better regulation mechanisms. People may explore neurofeedback when they are interested in focus, emotional control, sleep, stress resilience, or cognitive functioning.

Comparison Between Biofeedback and Neurofeedback: Main Differences

Although neurofeedback belongs to the broader family of biofeedback techniques, there are several distinctions.

First, the systems targeted in training are different. Biofeedback is about training physiological systems of the body, while neurofeedback deals with training the brain.

For instance, biofeedback can be useful for learning to regulate breathing or relax muscles, while neurofeedback focuses on brainwave activity.

Second, the technologies used are different. In biofeedback, sensors are placed on various parts of the body to track physiological reactions such as changes in heart rate, respiration, or muscle activity. In neurofeedback, electrodes are placed on the scalp to record the brain’s electrical activity.

Third, the objectives differ. Biofeedback is aimed at improving awareness and regulation of physiological reactions to stress, while neurofeedback focuses on supporting healthy brain regulation.

Similarities of Biofeedback Therapy and Neurofeedback Therapy

Even though the methods work with different parts of the body, they share several similarities. Both therapies are non-invasive and do not involve medication. Each session is held with a specialist who uses equipment to receive real-time feedback.

This type of training can help participants understand how their body responds to internal and external factors. Whether the goal is physical relaxation or improved brain activity, both approaches share a principle of increasing awareness of the body and its processes.

Kinds of Biofeedback

Electromyography (EMG) Biofeedback

EMG biofeedback measures the electrical activity and level of contraction of muscles. This type of biofeedback can help people gain awareness of muscle tension.

Heart Rate Variability (HRV) Biofeedback

HRV biofeedback examines the relationship between breathing and heart rate. It may be helpful for practicing stress management and supporting autonomic regulation.

Thermal Biofeedback

Thermal biofeedback evaluates changes in skin temperature that may be related to stress or relaxation. This can help a person understand the relationship between emotional state and blood flow.

Respiratory Biofeedback

Respiratory biofeedback evaluates breathing patterns and supports practice with breathing techniques.

Galvanic Skin Response (GSR)

GSR measures changes in the conductivity of the skin associated with sweating.

Brain Waves in Neurofeedback

Neurofeedback involves brainwave analysis through EEG technology. Brainwaves are associated with different states of mind and levels of activity.

Delta Waves

Delta waves are slow brain waves that occur during deep sleep.

Theta Waves

Theta activity is associated with creativity, relaxation, daydreaming, and light sleep.

Alpha Waves

Alpha waves are associated with relaxed and calm brain function.

Beta Waves

Beta waves usually occur when people are actively thinking and solving problems.

Gamma Waves

Gamma waves are associated with higher-level brain functions such as learning.

Contrary to what may be thought initially, neurofeedback is not aimed at creating one ideal brainwave pattern. It focuses on supporting balance and regulation of brain activity.

Potential Advantages of Biofeedback

Many people seek biofeedback as part of an overall wellness plan because it may help develop body awareness. Potential advantages may include:

  • Relaxation skills training
  • Stress management
  • Breathing awareness
  • Muscle relaxation
  • Healthy heart rate variability
  • General mind-body awareness

Results vary from person to person, and biofeedback is not meant to replace necessary medical treatment.

Potential Advantages of Neurofeedback

Neurofeedback is often sought by people interested in supporting brain function in daily life. Potential advantages may include:

  • Improved focus and concentration
  • Emotional control
  • Improved cognitive function
  • Sleep support
  • Resilience
  • Self-awareness
  • Stress management

Results vary individually and depend on many different factors.

For Whom Is Biofeedback Suitable?

Biofeedback may be considered by people who wish to learn how to manage physical stress reactions and practice self-regulation.

Some people consider biofeedback when they want support with:

  • Managing stress
  • Learning relaxation techniques
  • Breathing exercises
  • Muscle relaxation
  • Overall well-being

A medical practitioner can assess whether biofeedback may be appropriate for you.

For Whom Is Neurofeedback Suitable?

Neurofeedback may be considered by people who aim to support brain function and self-regulation.

Some people explore neurofeedback for goals such as:

  • Attention and focus
  • Emotional control
  • Cognitive performance
  • Quality of sleep
  • Resilience to stress
  • Performance

Because every brain is different, a personal assessment may be appropriate before starting training.

What Goes On in a Session?

Knowing what to expect can ease pre-session jitters.

In a Biofeedback Session

Sensors are applied to certain parts of the body to track physiological processes such as heartbeat, breathing, or muscle movements. The information appears on a screen while the client practices relaxation and breathing exercises.

In a Neurofeedback Session

Sensors are placed on the scalp to record brain activity in the form of brainwaves. The session may involve watching a movie, animated images, or games while a computer program provides feedback based on brain activity.

Is It Possible to Combine Biofeedback and Neurofeedback Sessions?

Depending on personal goals and requirements, some clinics offer both methods as part of a personalized wellness program. Biofeedback monitors physiological processes while neurofeedback works with brainwave activity.

Combining the techniques may provide a broader view of the interaction between mind and body. Whether this is appropriate depends on the client’s goals and the practitioner’s assessment.

Misconceptions About Biofeedback and Neurofeedback

Misconception: They Are One and the Same

Although neurofeedback is one type of biofeedback, traditional biofeedback tracks bodily functions such as respiration and muscular activity, while neurofeedback focuses on brainwave training.

Misconception: The Therapy Causes Discomfort

Both kinds of therapy are non-invasive. The electrodes and sensors monitor biological activity rather than electrically stimulating the body or brain.

Misconception: The Effects Are Instantaneous

Learning to regulate oneself takes practice. Many people need several sessions before noticing changes, and everyone’s experience is different.

Misconception: These Therapies Can Replace Medical Care

Biofeedback and neurofeedback are generally used as supportive therapy approaches. They should not replace medical assessment or treatment when those are needed.

Frequently Asked Questions

Could biofeedback and neurofeedback be combined?

Yes. Depending on personal goals and requirements, some practitioners use both approaches in customized programs.

Is neurofeedback a kind of biofeedback?

Yes. Neurofeedback is a particular type of biofeedback that focuses exclusively on brainwaves and uses EEG technology.

Is the procedure painful?

No. Both techniques are non-invasive and usually involve placing electrodes or sensors on the body or head.

What is the duration of a session?

It differs depending on the practitioner and treatment course, but many sessions take approximately 30 to 60 minutes.

Do I have to undergo brain mapping before starting neurofeedback?

Some centers conduct QEEG brain mapping before neurofeedback. A provider can explain whether an assessment is appropriate for your goals.

Concluding Remarks

Although biofeedback and neurofeedback use the same basic concept of feedback, they are applied differently. Biofeedback teaches people about the regulation of breathing, heartbeat, and muscles, while neurofeedback focuses on brain activity.

Making the right choice depends on your objectives and the guidance of a specialist. If you would like to learn more about these techniques, scheduling an appointment may be helpful.
