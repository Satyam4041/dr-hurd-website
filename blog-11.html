Why Is It So Hard to Concentrate? Understanding Attention and Brain Function

Nov 18, 2026·7 min read

By Dr. Merlyn Hurd, PhD

Have you ever started a task you planned to do, only to find yourself drifting off after a few minutes? Have you re-read the same paragraph several times, forgotten what someone just said, or struggled to stay focused on what matters?

Trouble concentrating is common, but when it happens all the time, it can affect your work, education, family relationships, and daily activities.

Understanding why you have difficulty focusing is the first step in the right direction. At NY Neurofeedback, Dr. Merlyn Hurd and the team offer personalized care, which can include QEEG brain mapping and other services.

What Happens in the Brain When You Concentrate?

Concentration relies on several brain functions working together. Your brain needs to pay attention to relevant information, cope with distractions, remember information, and switch between tasks when needed.

These abilities are linked to executive functions, which help people plan, organize, make decisions, and manage daily routines.

When these functions are under strain, even simple tasks can feel very difficult. You may find yourself checking your phone constantly, forgetting what others say, or being unable to finish a task that used to be easy.

Attention is not controlled by one specific part of the brain. Several brain networks interact, which is why problems with concentration can have many different causes.

Why Is It So Difficult to Stay Focused?

There is no single reason why concentration problems occur. Here are several factors that can affect attention.

1. Stress and Cognitive Overload

It becomes hard to concentrate when multiple deadlines, concerns, and tasks are on your mind. You may be sitting down to work while your mind runs through a list of other thoughts. Long-term stress can also affect sleep and mood, making it even harder to concentrate.

2. Lack of Good Sleep

Sleep plays an important role in memory, attention, and brain function. Disrupted sleep patterns, insomnia, and other sleep problems can leave you feeling sleepy during the day.

If you have trouble concentrating even when you are getting plenty of sleep, talk with your doctor.

3. Constant Digital Distractions

A May 2026 article from Stanford Medicine highlighted how constant digital interruptions and the pull of new information can make sustained attention more challenging.

Taking breaks from notifications and focusing on one task at a time may help create a more supportive environment for concentration.

4. Anxiety and Emotional Concerns

Anxiety can use up mental energy that would otherwise go toward daily activities. Overthinking, constant worrying, and emotional difficulties can make it hard to listen to others, read, or do your job.

When attention problems appear together with ongoing anxiety or mood changes, consulting a psychologist may be a good idea.

5. ADHD and Attention Problems

Attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) is a neurodevelopmental condition characterized by a pattern of inattention and/or hyperactivity-impulsivity. Common attention problems include:

  • Struggling to finish tasks
  • Misplacing or forgetting everyday objects
  • Difficulty organizing and managing time
  • Getting distracted easily
  • Difficulty following instructions or conversations

However, trouble paying attention does not necessarily mean ADHD. Sleep disorders, anxiety, depression, and some learning or medical issues can cause similar difficulties. A comprehensive evaluation is necessary.

How Can QEEG Brain Mapping Help to Understand Brain Activity?

Quantitative electroencephalography (QEEG), also called QEEG brain mapping, records and analyzes the brain’s electrical activity. These activity patterns can provide additional information during an individualized evaluation.

At NY Neurofeedback, QEEG brain mapping may be used as one step in understanding your concerns and planning appropriate care.

QEEG is not a stand-alone diagnostic tool for ADHD, anxiety, or other psychological conditions. Its results should be interpreted together with your clinical history and other assessments. If you are looking for QEEG brain mapping in NYC, understanding what this test can and cannot tell you is an important step.

What Is the Role of Neurofeedback in Attention Training?

Neurofeedback is a brain training technique based on real-time feedback about the brain’s electrical activity.

Sensors are placed on the scalp to measure brain activity, and a computer program gives visual or audio feedback. For example, a person may watch a movie or listen to music during a session while the system responds to their brain activity.

Scientists have studied neurofeedback for attention problems such as ADHD, but the results are mixed. A 2025 systematic review and meta-analysis of 38 randomized clinical trials with 2,472 participants found no significant effect of neurofeedback as a stand-alone treatment for ADHD.

Neurofeedback can therefore be considered as one option, with clear limitations.

Frequently Asked Questions About Concentration and Brain Function

Why can’t I concentrate even when I want to?

Stress, insomnia, anxiety, digital distractions, ADHD, and other issues can interfere with concentration. Looking at the possible reasons is more helpful than blaming a single factor.

Does the inability to concentrate mean ADHD?

No. There are many reasons for attention problems. An ADHD diagnosis requires a thorough evaluation by a qualified professional.

Can QEEG brain mapping detect attention disorders?

QEEG brain mapping records and analyzes the brain’s electrical activity, but it cannot diagnose ADHD or pinpoint the cause of concentration difficulties on its own.

Can neurofeedback help to improve attention?

Neurofeedback is being researched for various attention problems. Research results are mixed, and neurofeedback should not be presented as an answer to all concentration issues.
